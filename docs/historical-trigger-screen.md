## Strongest new lead: H100 SXM5 64 GB HBM2e

NVIDIA's 2023 R525 release notes explicitly list partner HGX H100 4-way 64 GB HBM2e and 94 GB HBM2e, alongside 80 GB HBM3 [S60]. April 2026 hardware support notes repeat the two HBM2e SKUs [S64, page image checked]; the vGPU reference distinguishes physical board categories [S61]. This is not just a small virtual GPU profile. The full GH100 has six HBM interface equivalents [S62]. Four, five, or six real 16 GB-class stacks would imply 64, 80, or 96 GB-class physical totals respectively, but effective width, actual populated stacks, density, health, and market quantity for the 64 GB board are unknown. An ordinary four-stack configuration, structural spacers or disabled faulty components remain strong alternatives. Compare 64/94 GB HBM2e package groups; secondary PCI identifiers 2337/2339 are search leads [S78], not proof of donor origin.

## Actual supply shocks

NVIDIA's May 2025 CFO commentary describes a USD 4.5 billion H20 charge following the April 9 licensing notice, reduced by reuse of some materials [S65]. The 10-Q identifies USD 1.9 billion of H20 product inventory provision; the balance is excess purchase-obligation liabilities [S66]. The following quarter recorded USD 180 million provision release on H20 products sold outside China [S67]. These are candidate-specific evidence of inventory stress, material reuse and changed sales destinations, not evidence that any specific HBM or completed package moved to another SKU. Accounting figures do not count boards. The official H20A 96 GB HBM3 and H20 141 GB HBM3e configurations [S64,S77] suggest comparing dated assemblies; the latter's density does not imply the former physically contains that much. A nominal 144 GB-class construction requires six actual 24 GB-class stacks and compatibility/partial exposure. Ordinary lower-density HBM3 is a stronger simple explanation. No post-April-2025 favorable lot is identified.

AMD reports MI308-specific licensing disruption and about USD 440 million net inventory-related charges in fiscal 2025; some licensed shipments began at fiscal year end [S68]. AMD identifies MI308X 1002:74a2 [S69] and first-person reports describe 192 GB MI308X devices [S70]. Do not convert a rumor about a smaller MI308 configuration into a 96 GB MI308X baseline. Keep as a supply watch until a real lower-capacity SKU and population are identified. No export-control circumvention is proposed.

## Additional late-family and geometry leads

A800 Active 40 GB has official HBM2/5120-bit/1555.2 GB/s [S71]. Its late product lineage motivates a package-reassignment hypothesis, but an ordinary five-by-8 GB configuration suffices. A 48 GB scenario requires a sixth real 8 GB-class stack; an 80 GB scenario requires higher density and partial exposure. Two linked cards are not one 80 GB card. H100 80 GB has a six-interface architecture and five active stacks; a launch-period physical report described the sixth surrounding package as yield/structural support [S62,S63]. Its population is not proven live DRAM. The official H100 96 GB comparator is separately named [S64].

H100 NVL 94 GB has 6016 data lanes and 3938 GB/s [S72, page image checked]. Relative to a six-stack 6144-lane, 96 GB-class reference, both width and capacity are 47/48. This is consistent with proportional channel reduction rather than a large capacity-only anomaly. Not a positive healthy-memory claim.

Blackwell B200 180 versus nominal 192 GB [S73] and B300 270 versus 288 GB [S64,S74] may reflect accounting, revision, yield-related exclusions, or different configurations; require exact bytes and same SKU before attributing them to physical excess. Both nominal label ratios are 93.75%, not proof of a specific partition mechanism. GB300 Desktop/DGX Station 252 GB and 7.1 TB/s versus full 288 GB and 8 TB/s [S74,S75] is roughly proportional (87.5% capacity, 88.75% bandwidth); seven actual 36 GB-class stacks can account for it. Do not include Grace CPU memory. Blackwell low-yielding material provisions [S67] make defective-resource harvesting a meaningful counterhypothesis.

AMD Alveo 8/16 GB with different pseudo-channel capacities and distinct FPGA designations [S76] is an ordinary comparator, outside the core GPU scope. Existing Intel Max 1100, CMP, and AMD Vega/MI50 leads remain in the previous cross-vendor ranking. This focused new-lead order is not a single calibrated ordering over all devices. Hardware support does not establish actual sales or volume. No new verified transactions or original measurements are presented.
